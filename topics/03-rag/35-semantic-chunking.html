Section 6 · Lesson 35

Semantic Chunking

The first advanced technique. RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter cuts by size; semantic chunking cuts by meaning: sentences about the same thing stay together, unrelated ones go into separate chunks.

The problem with size-based splitting

A size-based splitter (Lesson 10) only looks at chunk size and overlap, never at what the text says:

LangChain is a framework to build RAG applications.
LangChain can also be used to build agentic AI applications.
Paris is the capital of France.

Depending on the settings, the two LangChain sentences may land in different chunks, or one may share a chunk with the Paris sentence. A chunk that mixes topics produces a blurry vector that matches neither topic well.

Definition

Semantic chunking splits documents into meaningful chunks based on semantic similarity, not just the number of tokens or lines. The goal: chunks that are self-contained, contextually rich and logically separated, which means better retrieval and better answers.

How it works

  1. SegmentSplit the document into sentences S1, S2, S3…, e.g. with a regular expression at each full stop.
  2. EmbedTurn each sentence into a vector (OpenAI or a Hugging Face sentence transformer).
  3. Compare neighboursCosine similarity between adjacent sentences: S1–S2, S2–S3, S3–S4…
  4. Merge or splitAbove a threshold → same chunk; below → start a new chunk.

The threshold (75%? 80%? 95%?) has no universal answer; set it with a domain expert for your data. Higher means sentences must be more alike to merge.

Example

"LangChain is a framework for building LLM-powered applications. It integrates with tools like OpenAI and Pinecone. The Eiffel Tower is located in Paris. France is a popular destination." → the two LangChain sentences merge into one chunk; the Eiffel Tower and France sentences stay out of it.

Recursive splitterSemantic chunking
Splits bySize and separatorsSimilarity between sentences
Main settingchunk_size, chunk_overlapSimilarity threshold
ChunksEven sizes; may mix or split topicsUneven sizes; one topic each
CostFree and instantOne embedding per sentence at ingestion: slower and, with paid models, costlier

Since only neighbours are compared, a topic that comes back later in the document starts a new chunk rather than rejoining the earlier one.

Takeaways

  • Size-based splitting ignores meaning, so chunks can mix or split topics.
  • Semantic chunking: segment → embed → compare neighbours → merge above the threshold.
  • Trade-off: cleaner chunks, at the cost of embedding every sentence.
