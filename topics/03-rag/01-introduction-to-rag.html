Section 1 · Lesson 1

Why RAG: The Problem with LLMs

An LLM (large language model: an AI trained on huge amounts of text to understand and write language, like GPT, Llama or Gemini) only knows what was in its training data. RAG, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, fixes that by letting it look things up before it answers. More than 80% of business use cases in generative AI today are RAG applications, which makes it a must-know.

Limit 1: the training cut-off

An LLM trained on data up to July 2023 answers questions about that data well. Ask it "What is today's AI news?" and it can't: it has never seen anything newer.

Limit 2: private data

An employee of company XYZ asks the company chatbot "What is XYZ's latest leave policy?" No LLM was trained on XYZ's internal documents, and a web search won't find them either.

What happens when an LLM doesn't know: hallucination

A chef in India (the LLM) was trained mostly on Indian dishes. A customer orders a European dish the chef has never learned. The chef can:

Chef's optionResultLLM equivalent
Guess the recipe and cook anywayTerrible foodHallucination
Say "I don't know this dish"No food, but honest"I don't know"
Look it up in a recipe book and follow itA great dishRAG

Hallucination: when an LLM doesn't have the knowledge but makes up a confident-sounding answer anyway.

It happens because an LLM generates the most plausible-sounding text; it doesn't check facts. Without the right information in front of it, plausible and true can differ.

The fix: an open-book exam

RAG improves an LLM by combining its ability to generate text with retrieving knowledge from an external source.

Plain LLMLLM with RAG
Like a student in a…Closed-book examOpen-book exam
Can useOnly what it memorisedWhat it memorised + reference material
ResultLimited to its training; may guessAccurate, detailed, up-to-date answers

The reference material can be current information or a company's private documents, solving both limits above.

Takeaways

  • LLMs only know their training data: nothing after the cut-off, nothing private.
  • When they don't know, they may hallucinate: confident, made-up answers.
  • RAG lets the LLM look up the right information first: an open-book exam.
