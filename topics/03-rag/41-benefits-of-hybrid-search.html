Section 7 · Lesson 41

Benefits of Hybrid Search

Why run two searches? Because each covers the other's blind spots.

Sparse (BM25) is strong at

  • Exact terms, names, codes
  • Rare keywords: BM25 weighs them heavily (crucial in technical, legal and medical text, e.g. "osteoporosis")
  • Users who search by specific terms ("LangChain agents")

Dense (embeddings) is strong at

  • Synonyms and rephrasing ("create app" ↔ "build system")
  • Descriptive questions ("How do I use LangChain to talk to tools?") and loosely written text
  • Typos and spelling variants

Together they boost recall (fewer relevant documents missed), cope with mixed collections of well-structured and messy documents, and are easy to tune with the weights.

Where it pays off

  • Avoiding "retrieval hallucination": retrieving chunks that don't really answer the question. Requiring keyword and meaning matches guards against it.
  • Technical docs: "how to use the API" vs docs titled "API usage".
  • Legal and medical Q&A: precise terms and varied wording.
  • Product search: "cheap noise cancelling headphones" ↔ "affordable ANC earbuds": dense connects the meaning, BM25 confirms "ANC".
  • Multilingual retrieval and support transcripts.

Takeaways

  • Keywords vs meaning: hybrid search gets both.
  • Best fits: technical, legal, medical and product search.
