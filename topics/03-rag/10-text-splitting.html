Section 3 · Lesson 10

Text Splitting

Loaded documents can be huge, so the next ingestion step is the text splitter: it cuts documents into chunks. This lesson covers LangChain's three common splitters and the settings that control them.

The settings

from langchain.text_splitter import (
    CharacterTextSplitter,
    RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter,
    TokenTextSplitter,
)

text = documents[0].page_content   # the loaded machine_learning.txt
SettingMeaning
separator(s)Where the text may be cut, e.g. a new line ("\n") or a space (" ").
chunk_sizeThe maximum size of a chunk.
chunk_overlapHow much of the end of one chunk may repeat at the start of the next.
length_functionHow size is measured; len counts characters.

Every splitter has split_text(text) (string in, list of strings out; used in this lesson) and split_documents(docs) (Documents in, Documents out, so each chunk keeps its metadata).

CharacterTextSplitter

Cuts at one separator, then packs the pieces into chunks no bigger than chunk_size.

char_splitter = CharacterTextSplitter(
    separator="\n",       # cut at new lines
    chunk_size=200,       # max 200 characters per chunk
    chunk_overlap=20,
    length_function=len,
)
char_chunks = char_splitter.split_text(text)
print(len(char_chunks))   # 4

4 chunks, one per section of the text. No overlap appears despite chunk_overlap=20 (see below).

RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter

Tries a list of separators in order, cutting at the biggest natural break that fits. The recommended default.

recursive_splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
    separators=["\n\n", "\n", " ", ""],   # tried in this order
    chunk_size=200,
    chunk_overlap=20,
    length_function=len,
)
recursive_chunks = recursive_splitter.split_text(text)
print(len(recursive_chunks))   # 6

6 smaller, cleaner chunks. It splits at paragraphs ("\n\n") first; any piece still too big is split at lines ("\n"), then words (" "), and only as a last resort mid-word (""). That's why it respects the text's structure.

When overlap actually appears

The rule

Overlap is made of whole pieces (the bits between separators). If each piece is a whole line longer than chunk_overlap, none fits, so nothing repeats. Overlap shows up when pieces are small: words (" ") or characters (""). 

simple_text = "This is sentence one and it is quite long. This is sentence two and it is also ..."

splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
    separators=[" "],     # only split at spaces (words)
    chunk_size=80,
    chunk_overlap=20,
    length_function=len,
)
chunks = splitter.split_text(simple_text)

for i in range(len(chunks) - 1):
    print(f"Chunk {i + 1} ends:   ...{chunks[i][-20:]}")
    print(f"Chunk {i + 2} starts: {chunks[i + 1][:20]}...")

Splitting only at spaces, the last few words of each chunk ("and it is also") start the next one. CharacterTextSplitter with separator="" does the same at the character level.

TokenTextSplitter

Measures size in tokens (the pieces an LLM splits text into, usually a word or part of a word) instead of characters.

token_splitter = TokenTextSplitter(chunk_size=50, chunk_overlap=10)
token_chunks = token_splitter.split_text(text)

At most 50 tokens per chunk, 10 overlapping. It counts tokens the way a model does (with tiktoken), so chunks fit token limits exactly. (The video describes tokens as words and spaces; a token is really the model's own unit.)

Which to use

SplitterProsConsUse when
CharacterSimple, predictableMay cut mid-sentenceText has one clear delimiter
RecursiveCharacterRespects structure; best general-purposeSlightly more complexDefault for most text
TokenSizes chunks the way the model countsSlowerToken-limited models

Why overlap matters

The answer to a question doesn't always sit inside one chunk: part of it may end one chunk and its context start the next. Overlap puts that boundary text in both, so each retrieved chunk still makes sense on its own.

In practice you try different chunk_size and chunk_overlap values for your data: too small and chunks lose context, too large and they blur several topics into one vector.

Takeaways

  • Settings: separators, chunk_size, chunk_overlap, length_function.
  • RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter is the default: paragraphs → lines → words → characters.
  • Overlap only appears with small pieces, and keeps boundary context in both chunks.
  • TokenTextSplitter sizes chunks in the model's own tokens.
