Lesson 9

Renewals, failures and cancellations

The first payment is the start of a relationship. After that, money moves every month without the customer present, charges sometimes fail, people cancel from their UPI app, and someone has to switch access off at the right moment.

A subscription's life, month by month

The one number that matters: paid-until

Lots of things happen to a subscription: approvals, charges, failures, retries, pauses, cancellations. You can get lost tracking all of them. Instead, keep one simple fact per subscription:

"This customer has paid for tier X until date Y."

  • Every successful payment moves paid-until forward by exactly one period.
  • Nothing else moves it. Not a status change, not a failed charge, not a cancellation.
  • Access is simply: "today is before paid-until".

The gateway's status (ACTIVE, ON_HOLD, CANCELLED…) tells you about future charges. Paid-until tells you about access. Keep them separate.

Renewals: one payment, one period

For periodic plans the gateway charges automatically each cycle and sends a webhook (SUBSCRIPTION_PAYMENT_SUCCESS with type CHARGE). Handling it:

  1. Check the signature, store the event As in Lesson 8.
  2. Insert the payment into the ledger by its payment ID (UNIQUE) If the insert is a duplicate, this payment was already counted: stop here.
  3. Only for a new payment: extend paid-until by one period Ideally in the same database transaction as the insert, so you never get "recorded but not extended".
  4. Optionally confirm with the gateway Fetch the payment or subscription to double-check the amount and status.

Extend from the period end, not from "now"

❌ paid-until = now + 1 month

The charge runs on 3 Mar because of a retry. Access now runs to 3 Apr, and the next one to 3 May… The customer slowly gets extra days, and your dates drift away from the gateway's billing dates. If a charge comes in early, they lose days.

✅ paid-until = old paid-until + 1 month

1 Mar + 1 month = 1 Apr, no matter when the charge actually landed. Your dates stay in step with billing. (If paid-until is long in the past, e.g. after a long gap, restart from the payment date instead.)

Edge case: AUTH is not a renewal

The approval-time payment (AUTH) also arrives as "payment success". It's the first grant, not a renewal, and the first grant usually already happened in verify. Treat AUTH as "grant if not yet granted" and only CHARGE as "extend one period".

Failed charges and ON_HOLD

Monthly charges fail for ordinary reasons: low balance, bank downtime, an expired card, a revoked UPI mandate.

  • The gateway usually retries on its own for a few days. Meanwhile the subscription may show ON_HOLD.
  • Record each failed attempt in the ledger (with the failure reason). Useful for support and for messages to the customer.
  • Don't cut access immediately. Paid-until hasn't passed yet, so the customer still has what they paid for.
  • Tell the customer. "Your payment for March didn't go through. Please check your bank or UPI app." Chasing failed payments is called dunning.
  • Decide a grace period (e.g. 3–7 days after paid-until) during which access continues while retries happen. It's a product choice; write it down.
  • If a retry succeeds → it's a normal renewal (new payment ID → extend from the old paid-until).
  • If retries are exhausted → the subscription may be cancelled by the gateway; access ends at paid-until (+ grace).

Cancellations: two doors

A subscription can be cancelled from your app or from outside it. Both must end in the same state.

Door 1: "Cancel" in your app

  1. Check the subscription belongs to this user and is actually cancellable (not a trial, not already cancelled, not never-started).
  2. Call the gateway's cancel API (e.g. Manage Subscription → CANCEL).
  3. On success, mark it cancelled and record cancelledAt.
  4. Tell the user: "Auto-pay stopped. You keep access until 1 Apr."

Door 2: outside your app

The customer revokes the mandate in their UPI app or bank, which is their legal right in India. Or the gateway cancels after failed retries.

You only find out through a webhook (status changed → CUSTOMER_CANCELLED / CANCELLED). Same result: stop expecting charges, keep access until paid-until.

  • Cancelling twice is harmless: the second attempt finds it already cancelled, so reply "already cancelled", not an error.
  • Cancel API fails? Don't mark it cancelled locally, or the customer is still charged while your app says they aren't. Show an error and let them retry.
  • The cancel webhook arrives after your own cancel: it's a duplicate in effect. No change needed.
  • Cancelling something that never started (still INITIALIZED): just mark it abandoned; there's no access to keep.
Watch out: cancel ≠ refund

Cancelling stops future charges. It doesn't return money already paid. Refunds are a separate, deliberate action with their own API and rules (a later topic).

Pauses and expired cards

  • Customer paused (CUSTOMER_PAUSED): no charges while paused. Treat like a soft cancel: access until paid-until; resumes on the next successful charge.
  • Card expired (CARD_EXPIRED): card mandates die with the card. Access until paid-until, then ask the customer to subscribe again with a new card or UPI. A "card expiring soon" reminder event lets you warn them earlier.

Turning access off

When paid-until passes, someone must actually remove access. Two approaches:

Check at access timeScheduled job
HowEvery feature check asks "is today before paid-until?"A job runs every hour or night and downgrades users whose paid-until has passed
AccuracyExact to the secondLate by up to one job interval
SpeedSlightly more work per checkChecks stay simple (just read the tier)
RiskMust be added to every checkIf the job stops, nobody is ever downgraded

A common combination: store the tier for fast checks, run a job to downgrade, and double-check paid-until in the backend for important features. Also alert if the job hasn't run.

Tip: make the expiry job safe to re-run

Write it as "set to the correct tier for everyone whose paid-until has passed" rather than "downgrade by one step". Then running it twice, or after a crash, is harmless.

When access comes from more than one place

Real products give access from several sources: the customer's own subscription, a free trial, a family member's plan, a promo, an older subscription still within its period.

Edge case: one source ending must not cancel the others

If any event (a failed new attempt, an expiring trial, a cancelled old plan) simply sets the user to "free", it can wipe out access that's still valid from another source. Instead, when anything changes, recalculate access as "the best tier from all still-valid sources".

Free trials

  • No payment involved: a trial is just a local entitlement with an end date. Keep it in the same "source of access" model, with its own paid-until (trial-until).
  • One per person, forever: record that the trial was used, so cancelling and re-joining doesn't give another.
  • Nothing to cancel at the gateway: there's no mandate. "Cancel" on a trial is just "end now" or "let it run out".
  • Trial → paid: decide whether paid time starts now (trial days are lost) or after the trial ends (requires setting the first charge date). Either is fine if it's deliberate and explained.

Changing plans

Upgrades and downgrades deserve their own flow. The key rules:

  • Never end up with two live mandates for the same customer. They'd be billed twice.
  • A typical upgrade: create the new subscription → once it's active, cancel the old one → access switches to the higher tier.
  • Proration (credit for unused days on the old plan) is optional and complex. Many products skip it and simply start the new plan's period now, or at the old period's end.
  • A typical downgrade: takes effect at the end of the current paid period, so the customer gets what they paid for.
