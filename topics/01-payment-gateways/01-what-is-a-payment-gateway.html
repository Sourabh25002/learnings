Lesson 1

What is a payment gateway?

When a customer pays ₹499 on your website, at least five parties are involved in a few seconds. A payment gateway is the piece that lets you accept that money without ever touching the customer's card or bank details yourself.

The players

Every online payment involves these parties:

PlayerWhoTheir job
CustomerThe person payingChooses a method and approves (OTP, UPI PIN, netbanking login).
MerchantYouSays "charge this customer this amount for this thing", then delivers what was paid for.
Payment gatewayCashfree, Razorpay, Stripe, PayU…Collects payment details securely and routes the request into the banking system.
NetworkVisa, Mastercard, RuPay, or NPCI for UPI and NACHThe "roads" between banks. They pass the request to the right bank.
Issuing bankThe customer's bankChecks the balance or limit and the OTP/PIN, then says yes or no.
Acquiring bankThe bank on the merchant's sideReceives the money for the merchant. With an aggregator, it's hidden from you.

Think of sending a parcel. You (merchant) hand it to a courier company (gateway). The courier uses highways and sorting hubs (networks) to reach the receiver's local post office (issuing bank), which checks the receiver's ID before delivering. You never drive the truck yourself; you just get a tracking update.

Two flows: approval and money

A payment is really two separate journeys that happen at different speeds. Confusing them is a common beginner mistake.

Approval (authorization)

"Is this customer allowed to pay this amount?" Happens in real time. This is what your app cares about to give access.

Settlement

The actual money arriving in your bank, in batches, minus fees. This is what your accounts team cares about.

Watch out

"Payment successful" means approved, not "money is in my account". Refunds, chargebacks and failed settlements can still happen afterwards. That's why reconciliation exists (a later topic).

Gateway vs aggregator

People use "payment gateway" loosely, but there are two roles:

Payment gateway

Only the technology pipe. It moves payment data securely. The money goes to a merchant account you hold with an acquiring bank.

Payment aggregator

Pipe plus money handling. It collects funds into its own account on your behalf and settles them to you. One signup, no separate bank merchant accounts needed.

Cashfree, Razorpay and PayU are aggregators. That's why onboarding with them is quick: they've already done the bank paperwork. In India, the RBI licenses and regulates payment aggregators.

The gateway hides all of this behind one checkout. You don't integrate each method separately.

Why the gateway hosts the payment page

You almost never build your own card form. Instead, you send the customer to a checkout page or popup run by the gateway. Three reasons:

  1. Security rules (PCI DSS). Anyone who stores, processes or even sees card numbers must meet a strict, expensive security standard called PCI DSS. If card numbers only ever go to the gateway's page, your servers never see them, and almost all of that burden disappears.
  2. Complexity you don't want. OTP screens, UPI app switching, bank-specific netbanking flows, retries, bank downtime: the gateway handles all of it.
  3. Trust. Customers recognise gateway checkout pages and trust them with card details.

Your server starts the payment. The customer pays on the gateway's page. The gateway tells your server what happened. You never handle card or bank details.

What a gateway gives you

ToolWhat it's for
Server APIsCreate orders and subscriptions, fetch status, cancel, refund. Called from your backend with secret keys.
Checkout SDKA small JavaScript (or Android/iOS) library that opens the payment page using a session ID from your backend.
WebhooksThe gateway calls your server when something happens: success, failure, renewal, cancellation.
DashboardSee transactions, settlements, refunds and disputes; manage keys and webhook URLs.
SandboxA full test copy where no real money moves.

Sandbox vs production

Every gateway gives you two completely separate worlds:

Sandbox (test)Production (live)
MoneyFake. You simulate success or failure.Real.
API keysTest keysLive keys (different!)
Base URLe.g. sandbox.cashfree.come.g. api.cashfree.com
DataPlans, customers and orders exist only hereSeparate. Nothing copies over.
Edge cases
  • Mixed environments. Test keys against the live URL (or the reverse) give an authentication error (401). Keep the keys, base URL and environment name together in config, so they always switch together.
  • Setup doesn't copy over. Plans and webhook URLs created in sandbox don't exist in production. Recreate them before going live.
  • Test prices left in. Prices like ₹1 used for testing must be changed before launch. Put it on a go-live checklist.

Money words you'll hear

WordPlain meaning
MDRThe gateway's fee per payment, usually a percentage. UPI is often cheapest; credit cards cost most.
SettlementThe gateway paying your collected money into your bank, usually the next working day or two.
RefundYou send money back to the customer.
ChargebackThe customer complains to their bank, and the bank pulls the money back from you. You can respond with proof.
ReconciliationChecking that your database, the gateway's records and your bank statement all agree.
Tip: store money safely

Never store amounts as floating-point numbers. 0.1 + 0.2 is not exactly 0.3 in a float. Use a DECIMAL column, or store whole paise as an integer (₹499.00 = 49900), and always store the currency next to the amount.
