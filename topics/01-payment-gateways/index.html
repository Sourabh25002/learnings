Chapter 1

Payment Gateway Integration

Taking money online looks like one button. Behind it are banks, card networks, a gateway, your server, the customer's browser and a lot of things that can go wrong in between. This chapter builds the full mental model, then goes deep on every stage: creating an order safely, verifying it, handling webhooks, living with renewals and cancellations, and selling one-time add-ons.

What this chapter is (and isn't)

It's about ideas that apply to any payment integration: what each piece does, why it's designed that way, and every edge case worth knowing. It isn't a copy of one project's code. Examples use Indian payment methods and Cashfree's API names because that's where I learned it, but Razorpay, Stripe and others work the same way.

Glossary

Come back here whenever a term is unfamiliar.

Merchant
The business accepting the money. That's you.
Payment gateway
The technology that securely takes payment details from the customer and passes them to the banking system.
Payment aggregator
A gateway that also collects the money on your behalf and settles it to your bank account. Cashfree, Razorpay and PayU are aggregators. In India they're licensed by the RBI.
Issuing bank (issuer)
The customer's bank: the one that issued their card or holds their account.
Acquiring bank (acquirer)
The bank that receives payments on the merchant's side. With an aggregator you usually don't deal with it directly.
Card network
Visa, Mastercard, RuPay, Amex. They route card payments between acquirer and issuer.
NPCI
National Payments Corporation of India. It runs UPI, NACH and RuPay.
Mandate
The customer's standing permission for you to debit them again later without them being present. Needed for subscriptions.
UPI AutoPay
A mandate set up in a UPI app with the UPI PIN.
eNACH
An electronic mandate on a bank account, approved via netbanking, debit card or Aadhaar.
Plan
The template for a subscription: price, how often to charge and limits.
Order
A one-time payment request for a fixed amount.
Subscription
A recurring payment arrangement for one customer on one plan, backed by a mandate.
Session ID
A short-lived token the gateway gives you when you create an order or subscription. The browser uses it to open checkout.
Checkout
The gateway's payment page where the customer actually pays or approves.
Return URL
Where the gateway sends the customer's browser after checkout.
Webhook
A message the gateway's server sends to your server when something happens (payment success, failure, cancellation…).
Idempotency
Doing the same request twice has the same effect as doing it once.
Settlement
The gateway paying out collected money to your bank account, usually a day or two later.
MDR
Merchant Discount Rate: the fee taken from each payment.
Chargeback
The customer disputes a payment with their bank, and the money is pulled back from you.
Reconciliation
Matching your records against the gateway's and the bank's to make sure they agree.
Sandbox
The gateway's test environment. No real money moves.
PCI DSS
The security standard for anyone who handles card data.
HMAC signature
A fingerprint made from a message plus a shared secret. Lets you prove a webhook really came from the gateway and wasn't altered.
Replay attack
Re-sending a real, correctly signed message later to trigger the same effect again. Blocked by checking the timestamp.
Inbox table
Where you store every incoming webhook before processing it, so nothing is lost and duplicates are easy to spot.
Paid-until
The date up to which a customer has paid. Access is simply "today is before paid-until".
Renewal
A scheduled recurring charge (CHARGE) that extends paid-until by one period.
Dunning
Chasing failed recurring payments: notifying the customer and retrying.
Grace period
Extra days of access after paid-until while failed charges are retried.
Fulfilment
Delivering what was paid for: unlocking a feature, issuing a coupon, extending access.
Payment attempt
One try at paying an order. An order can have several; it's paid when one succeeds.
